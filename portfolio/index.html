Canvas LMS · 10 weeks · 40 educators · 2021

Developing Digital Citizenship

A virtual exchange for English teachers that still worked for every teacher who missed the live hour. 39 of 40teachers finished Read itFold it up

Audience and problem

  • Forty English teachers outside the US, most of them non-native English speakers
  • A ten-week virtual exchange, October to December 2021, across time zones
  • It had to build their skill at teaching digital citizenship and end in something they could use in their own classrooms, even for teachers who couldn’t make every live session

My role

Program designer and project manager.

  • I designed the program in the winning proposal. It won funding and was renewed twice on that design, around $140,000 in new grant revenue.
  • I set the course up on Canvas and wrote the Canvas orientation walkthrough.
  • I onboarded facilitators through three workshops (Canvas, engagement strategies, and module review with async plans), then ran weekly facilitator meetings.

Design decisions and why

  • One live hour, about three hours async: a teacher who missed the live session still had a complete week.
  • Pods of about ten per facilitator: feedback stayed personal at a distance.
  • A unit plan at the end: adults commit to learning they can put to work right away.
  • Tasks over open discussion: when the data showed task-based async activities drew more engagement, I shifted the recommendations for the next round toward tasks and a lighter weekly load.

What it looks like

Evidence

  • 39 of 40teachers finished
  • 80%earned certificates (31 teachers)
  • About 92%met or exceeded objectives on the funder’s post-survey
  • 56%average live attendance, so the async design carried the course for everyone who couldn’t attend

The funder renewed the design twice.

Articulate Rise · 14-module program · 2026

Instructor training in Articulate Rise

Interactive modules for instructors of adult learners, built faster without changing a word of the source text. 6.87 → 4.48hours to build a module Read itFold it up

Audience and problem

  • Instructors of adult learners, in a fourteen-module training program
  • Each module is a Rise course in four chapters: Pre-Learning, Reading, a Small Group Case Study, and Questions to Guide Your Practice
  • The Reading chapter is a formatted textbook chapter whose text can’t change
  • The challenge: consistent, interactive modules at a pace the program could afford

My role

Instructional designer and developer on a subcontract.

  • I built three modules: Connecting with Adult Learners, Outcome-Based Education, and Creating a Culture of Feedback.

Design decisions and why

  • Pre-Learning does the most: it’s the most interactive chapter, so instructors draw on what they already know before they read. Adults learn by connecting new ideas to experience.
  • The reading stays as written: the chapter keeps the author’s words exactly and does its work through formatting, so it’s easy to scan.
  • AI drafts the whole module first: Claude Code “lets me focus on instructional design decisions rather than copy-pasting,” and gives me a bird’s eye view of the whole module.
  • Consistency from that view: formatting and photo choices stay consistent across chapters.
  • Custom blocks when Rise can’t: a coding agent builds them.

What it looks like

Evidence

  • 6.87 → 4.48hours to build a module, from my timesheet
  • About 35%less build time per module
  • 3modules delivered for review
Lesson plans · slides · Kahoot · 2025

Consent curriculum for middle school

A client’s comics, turned into standards-aligned lessons a health teacher can teach as written. 5sessions, each aligned to California standards Read itFold it up

Audience and problem

  • Health teachers for middle school and grade 9
  • The client had a set of comics and an approved curriculum outline, but no way for a teacher to walk into a classroom and teach with them
  • For a district to adopt the material, it had to align to California’s required standards

My role

Freelance curriculum designer.

  • I turned the client’s comics into a five-session scope and sequence for grades 7 and 8.
  • I wrote lesson plans for grades 7, 8 and 9, with slide decks, student worksheets and Kahoot quizzes, all aligned to the approved outline.

Design decisions and why

  • Standards in every session: each maps to the California Healthy Youth Act and the state’s Health Education Content Standards, because adoption depends on it.
  • One comic per session: the client’s content stays at the center.
  • Practice, not lecture: values cards, an anonymous question box, jigsaw, a true or false game, role-play, exit tickets and a personal action plan, so students practice and teachers get quick checks for understanding.
  • An action table in every plan: what the facilitator does beside what students do, so a teacher new to the topic can run the session as written.
  • Teacher-ready files, not an LMS course: that’s how health teachers actually work.

What it looks like

Evidence

There’s no learner data, so the evidence is scope and rigor.

  • 2grade bands
  • 5sessions
  • 3grades of lesson plans
  • Everysession aligned to the standards
LearnWorlds, then HighLevel · 5 weeks · 2026

AI bootcamp for digital marketers

A beginner AI course where every learner leaves with a portfolio of things they built. 50units in 21 sections, over five weeks Read itFold it up

Audience and problem

  • Very beginner digital marketers learning to use AI in their daily work, with ChatGPT as the main platform
  • They needed to build real things
  • The course had to work for people who couldn’t attend live

My role

Curriculum designer and builder.

  • I designed the five-week second version.
  • I built the school on LearnWorlds, 50 units in 21 sections, and I’m now moving it to HighLevel.

Design decisions and why

  • Async stands on its own: “somebody should be able to join the course and only be able to build everything from the asynchronous assets.”
  • The learner picks by hand: the core repurposing exercise splits extracting ideas from generating content, with the learner choosing in between, so judgment stays with the person, not the tool.
  • A portfolio by week five: a course buddy saves each week’s work, so learners finish with something to show.
  • Evaluation from day one: a survey, knowledge checks and a post-survey, organized by Kirkpatrick level.

What it looks like

Evidence

The cohort is running now. Until the post-survey, the evidence is the build and the plan to measure it.

  • 5weeks
  • 50units in 21 sections
  • 3evaluation points: a survey, knowledge checks and a post-survey

Qinghai, China · 2012 to 2015

Xiangbala Foundation, project manager

  • I designed job readiness programs for Tibetan youth and moved the foundation from counting participants to showing impact, which made it more competitive for grants.

  • The foundation’s language academy grew from 15 to 100 students.
