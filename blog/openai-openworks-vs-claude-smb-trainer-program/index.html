OpenAI's OpenWorks vs. Anthropic's Claude SMB Trainer program: what's different, and what it means for a small team

Both bring free, hands-on AI help to small businesses. OpenAI pays local experts to work one-on-one; Anthropic trains community groups to run its workshop.

Edited by Ligaya Beebe · November 30, 2026

How this post was made: a site bot drafted this from OpenAI's OpenWorks page and help center, Anthropic's Claude SMB Tour and Claude for Small Business updates, Anthropic's Claude Corps and Claude for Nonprofits announcements, OpenAI and Anthropic job postings and academy help pages, and local and trade press coverage, then ran revision passes. Ligaya Beebe reviewed and approved it before it went live. Nothing on this blog publishes itself.

The short answer: both programs bring free, hands-on AI help to small businesses, but they're built differently. OpenAI's OpenWorks pays local AI experts, as contractors, to work one-on-one with owners on a project the owner picks. Anthropic's Claude SMB Trainer Program trains organizations small businesses already trust, like chambers of commerce and community colleges, to run Anthropic's workshop in their own towns. Both companies already give away online courses. Now both are paying for people in the room, which tells you something about how teams actually learn this stuff.

TL;DR

  • OpenWorks (launched November 23, 2026) funds individual partners with a stipend of up to $8,000 a month to give small businesses free one-on-one help, plus three months of ChatGPT Business.
  • The Claude SMB Trainer Program certifies community organizations to deliver a free, hands-on Claude workshop. Attendees get a month of Claude Max.
  • Both target small businesses. Anthropic's nonprofit work is separate, and it includes Claude Corps, a fellowship that pays early-career fellows $85,000 a year.
  • Anthropic hasn't said publicly whether SMB trainers are paid or reimbursed.
  • The takeaway: free courses get people curious. Time at the keyboard with a person nearby is what gets a team to the moment it clicks.

What is OpenAI's OpenWorks?

OpenWorks is a U.S. pilot that pays local AI experts to help small businesses for free. On the program page, OpenAI says it's "building a nationwide network of OpenWorks Partners to provide free, hands-on AI support to small businesses," and that it funds and equips those partners "to work alongside small business owners on projects they choose." The page lists 20 pilot communities, from New York, Chicago, and Los Angeles to Fargo, Charleston, and Incline Village, Nevada.

It launched on November 23, 2026, with a post on X rather than a company blog post, according to The Independent AI and Live 5 News in Charleston.

For a business, OpenAI's page says participation is free and no technical experience is needed. You get one-on-one support from a partner, hands-on practice with the tools you already use, and three months of ChatGPT Business at no cost. The pilot is open to independently owned U.S. small businesses, including restaurants, retailers, service firms, and manufacturers. Software and SaaS companies, venture-backed businesses, and private equity-owned businesses can't join. OpenAI's help center adds that nonprofits and faith-based organizations are welcome to apply through the business form.

The help center also suggests owners come with one task they'd like to make easier, such as adding receipts to accounting software or managing follow-up emails. That's the model in a sentence: one real task, one person helping, in your own tools.

What is Anthropic's closest match?

For small businesses, it's the Claude SMB Trainer Program. It grew out of the Claude SMB Tour, a run of free half-day workshops Anthropic and its build partner Tenex held in 10 U.S. cities this spring. In Anthropic's write-up of the tour, Head of U.S. SMB Lina Ochman says more than 1,000 owners and operators came through, and each stop paired AI fluency training with a session where business owners "opened their laptops and automated something real in their business."

Anthropic then trained outside organizations to run that same workshop. In a LinkedIn post announcing the program, Eleanor Dorfman asked chambers of commerce, Small Business Development Centers, community colleges, and economic development groups to nominate one or two facilitators for in-person training. Once they qualify, Anthropic sends the workshop materials and keeps them current. AICOE, one of the participating organizations, writes that facilitators also complete an online course and assessment, and that approved organizations commit to running at least five free workshops by the end of 2026.

On November 24, 2026, Anthropic said more than 150 organizations it trained as Approved Claude SMB Trainers will run over 750 workshops in their own communities.

If you've heard Anthropic's nonprofit programs mentioned in the same breath, those are separate. Claude for Nonprofits offers discounts of up to 75% on Team and Enterprise plans plus a free AI Fluency for Nonprofits course. Claude Corps places paid, full-time fellows inside nonprofits for a year. And Anthropic is hiring someone to build a train-the-trainer program for nonprofit intermediaries, modeled on the same idea.

How are the two program models different?

The biggest difference is who does the helping. OpenAI pays individuals to do project work with one business at a time. Anthropic equips existing community organizations to teach a room.

Each has a trade-off. One-on-one help goes deep on your business, but it's limited by how many days a partner has. A workshop reaches far more people at once, but you leave with a first workflow, not a finished project.

Who gets paid in each program?

OpenAI is clear about it. The help center says partners are contractors, the program offers a stipend of up to $8,000 a month, and partners should plan for at least four full days a month with local businesses plus one monthly workshop. It doesn't say how the amount is calculated or how long it's paid.

For the Claude SMB Trainer Program, Anthropic hasn't said publicly whether trainers are paid or have expenses covered. None of Anthropic's public pages or posts mention a stipend. One approved trainer wrote on LinkedIn that he traveled to the San Francisco training "at my own expense." That's one person's experience, not a stated policy.

Anthropic does pay people in its nonprofit program. Claude Corps fellows get a full-time salary of $85,000 plus benefits to spend a year working in person at a host nonprofit. Anthropic committed an initial $150 million, and says at least 400 nonprofits will host fellows over the next 12 months.

Why pay for in-person help when the courses are free?

Both companies already give training away. OpenAI Academy offers free, self-paced courses to anyone with a ChatGPT account, with badges for passing an assessment. Claude Academy is free with a free Claude account and awards completion badges for passing quizzes. OpenAI notes that its badges and certificates of completion "are not certifications."

Yet both are now funding people. This looks a lot like the forward-deployed engineer model both companies use with big customers. OpenAI's FDE job posting describes leading deployments "alongside our most strategic customers," and Anthropic's Staff FDE posting calls for traveling to customer sites "to build in person with customers." Neither company calls OpenWorks partners or SMB trainers FDEs. Still, the comparison fits: a person sitting next to the team, working on its real problems, is what these programs pay for.

Anthropic's tour write-up gives the clearest reason. It says "what closed the gap was time on the keyboard with someone nearby to help." Across 635 exit surveys, the most-cited highlight was the guided build session, and the most common request was more of it. Nearly two-thirds of attendees asked for hands-on help putting AI to work after the workshop. Those are Anthropic's own survey results, not an independent study.

Here's Ligaya's view, from training non-technical teams: a free course is a fine place to get curious, but it rarely gets a team to the aha moment. That moment usually comes when someone sits with you, opens the tool you already pay for, and gets one real task off your plate. After that, people keep going on their own.

What does this mean for a small team?

  1. Check if OpenWorks covers your town. The pilot map lists 20 communities. If yours isn't on it, the help center says you can still apply to show interest, but OpenAI can't promise a partner will be available.
  2. Look for a Claude SMB workshop nearby. Ask your chamber of commerce, SBDC, or community college whether they're an approved trainer. Anthropic's November update says more community-run workshops are being announced.
  3. Bring one task, not a wish list. Both programs work best when you show up with one chore you'd like to make easier. This guide on how a 10-person company should start with AI agents walks through picking one.
  4. Plan for the end of the free trial. OpenWorks includes three months of ChatGPT Business, and Claude workshops include one month of Claude Max. Decide up front how you'll judge whether to keep paying. This post on telling whether anyone is using your ChatGPT licenses covers what to measure.
  5. Bring the people who'll do the work. If only the owner attends, the skills stay with the owner. The help is free, so bring the person who actually handles the task.

The bigger takeaway: the companies that make these tools are now paying for human-centered capacity building. It's a fair signal that buying licenses and pointing people to a course isn't enough for most teams.

FAQ

Is OpenWorks free for small businesses?

Yes. OpenAI's OpenWorks page says participation is free and no technical experience is needed. Businesses get one-on-one support from a local partner and three months of ChatGPT Business at no cost.

Can a nonprofit join OpenWorks?

OpenAI's help center says nonprofits and faith-based organizations are welcome to apply using the business form. The pilot is aimed at independently owned U.S. small businesses, and software, SaaS, venture-backed, and private equity-owned companies aren't eligible.

Are Claude SMB trainers paid by Anthropic?

Anthropic hasn't said publicly. None of its public program pages or posts mention a stipend or expense coverage. One approved trainer wrote on LinkedIn that he traveled to the training at his own expense.

Is Claude Corps the same as the Claude SMB Trainer Program?

No. Claude Corps is a paid, year-long fellowship that places early-career fellows full time inside nonprofits, with a salary of $85,000 plus benefits. The Claude SMB Trainer Program trains community organizations to run free Claude workshops for small businesses.

What if OpenWorks isn't in my city?

OpenAI's help center says you can still apply as a business to show interest, but it can't promise OpenWorks will launch in your area or that a partner will be available. You can also ask your local chamber of commerce or Small Business Development Center whether it runs Claude SMB workshops.
